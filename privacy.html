Privacy & retention

Your camera. Your copies. Your policy.

Showing you what your own camera recorded is ordinary assistance. The important question is what happens after Muse fetches a snapshot or clip—and who can access it.

1

Scope

This guide assumes cameras you own or are authorized to operate. It is not permission to access someone else’s camera or footage.

2

Copies

Once Muse fetches a snapshot or clip and stores it elsewhere, Ring’s cloud encryption policy no longer defines the retention of that copy.

3

Access

Keep account credentials, refresh tokens, stored images, and automation logs away from public repositories and public static hosting.

TAKE, without the marketing fog

Ring’s 2026 TAKE direction may delete video keys after a window, but it is not end-to-end encryption. The server can still receive keys temporarily for smart alerts, search, or descriptions, and keys may be sent back if an old video is revisited.

For Muse, the practical rule is straightforward: state your own retention policy. Decide whether fetched clips are deleted after analysis, retained for a defined number of days, or never stored at all.

Suggested default for a template.Do not store snapshots or clips longer than the user needs. Do not put tokens in HTML, JavaScript, Git, or a public Pages deployment. Keep this static guide informational; any real connector needs a private backend and a documented access model.

Hosting notes

  • GitHub Pages and Cloudflare Pages are public static hosting environments. They are appropriate for this documentation site, not for private camera footage.
  • Use HTTPS and keep external payment widgets isolated to the support page.
  • Review vendor terms, local recording laws, and the people captured by your own cameras.
